A useful revision order

  1. Read the chapter page once, slowly, including the worked examples.
  2. Redo the journal entries on paper with the amounts covered.
  3. Attempt the practice questions before opening the answers.
  4. Finish with the formula sheet and the entry reference.
  5. Test yourself with the 80-question practice exam.
Practice exam · 80 MCQs

Practice exam, Parts 1–4

80 multiple-choice questions, 20 per part. Take all of them or one part at a time, then submit for a score by part and an explanation for every answer.

 Part 1 · Chapter 5

Cash and Receivables

Cash control, bank reconciliation, sales discounts and returns, the allowance method, aging, notes receivable, and receivables turnover.

 Part 2 · Chapter 6

Inventories

Perpetual and periodic systems, cost flow, FIFO, LIFO and weighted average, inventory errors, lower of cost or NRV, and turnover.

 Part 3 · Chapter 7

PPE and Intangible Assets

Acquisition cost, lump-sum purchases, repairs versus improvements, three depreciation methods, impairment, disposals, and amortization.

 Part 4 · Chapter 8

Liabilities

Accounts payable, notes and interest accruals, provisions and warranties, mortgages, and bonds at par, discount, and premium. Issue prices and mortgage payments are given, not calculated.

What this guide stays inside

The summaries follow the four lecture decks. Textbook wording is used only to make a rule the slides already teach easier to apply (for example, how a debit balance in the allowance changes the adjusting entry). Topics the slides do not teach are not turned into extra chapters. In particular:

  • Chapter 7 names natural resources on the chapter map and names fixed asset turnover in the learning outcomes. The slides do not teach depletion, and they do not compute fixed asset turnover. The formula sheet gives the standard ratio in one line and says so.
  • Part 4 keeps bond and mortgage concepts and the journal entries. The outline says you will not calculate bond prices or mortgage payments, so those computations are collapsed and marked not examined. Early extinguishment and bonds issued between interest dates are still absent, because the slides do not teach them.
  • LIFO is taught, and the slides also state that IFRS does not allow LIFO. Both points are kept.
  • The allowance chapter requires an expected-loss (allowance) model. A direct write-off of bad debts as they occur is marked as not acceptable.
