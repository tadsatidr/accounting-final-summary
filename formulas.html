Part 1 · Chapter 5 Cash and Receivables

Correct cash (bank) = bank balance + deposits in transit − outstanding checks ± bank errors

Hunt: 14,422 + 3,100 − 937 = 16,585. Outstanding checks = 326 + 426 + 185. A voided check is omitted.

Correct cash (books) = book balance + unrecorded receipts − unrecorded charges ± company errors

Hunt: 13,937 + 60 + 3,200 − 7 − 425 − 180 = 16,585.

Cash paid inside a discount = receivable × (1 − discount rate)

2/10, n/30 on 2,000: 2,000 × 0.98 = 1,960. Discount = 40, a contra-revenue.

Net sales = gross sales − sales discounts − sales returns and allowances

Bakery combined statement: 5,000 − 30 − 1,500 = 3,470. The 30 is 2% of the credit sales that were not returned.

Target allowance (percentage of receivables) = ending accounts receivable × estimated uncollectible percent

500,000 × 12% = 60,000. Expense = target − existing credit balance. With a 20,000 credit balance, expense = 40,000.

Aging: required allowance = Σ (bucket balance × that bucket’s loss percent)

Lecture total 5,897. With an existing credit balance of 2,000, expense = 3,897. Second schedule: required 35,294. After a 29,700 credit beginning balance and write-offs of 14,106, unadjusted allowance = 15,594 and expense = 19,700.

Net receivables = accounts receivable − allowance for bad debts

A write-off reduces both the receivable and the allowance. Net receivables do not change. 500,000 − 45,000 = 485,000 − 30,000 = 455,000.

Percentage of credit sales (Exercise 5 only): bad debt expense = credit sales × percent

1,300,000 × 1% = 13,000. This method does not solve for a target allowance balance.

Interest on a note = face × annual rate × time

HoHo: 5,000 × 12% × 3/12 = 150. Maturity value = 5,150. The 90-day note is measured as three months.

Accounts receivable turnover = Sales revenue ÷ Average accounts receivable

Average collection period = 365 ÷ Receivables turnover

Skechers 2023: turnover 9.36. Collection period = 365 / 9.36 = 39.0 days. The numerator in the slides is sales revenue.

Part 2 · Chapter 6 Inventories

Net purchases = purchases + freight-in − purchase returns − purchase discounts

Grantsville: 15,400 + 970 − 300 − 194 = 15,876. Shirt discount = 2% × 9,700 = 194.

Cost of goods sold = beginning inventory + net purchases − ending inventory

Harley-Davidson Motorclothes: 40,000 + 55,000 − 35,000 = 60,000. Periodic Grantsville with a 5,950 count: 15,876 − 5,950 = 9,926.

Perpetual shrinkage = inventory per the records − inventory per the physical count

6,776 − 5,950 = 826. A periodic system does not compute this as its own entry.

Ending inventory overstated by X → this year’s COGS understated by X, income overstated by X

Next year the same X overstates beginning inventory, overstates COGS, and understates income

Salina: a 2,000 overstatement moves 2016 income from 15,000 to 17,000 and 2017 income from 10,000 to 8,000. An understatement reverses every arrow.

Weighted-average unit cost = cost of goods available ÷ units available

560 / 7 = $80. COGS = 4 × 80 = 320. Ending inventory = 3 × 80 = 240.

FIFO: oldest costs → COGS; newest costs → ending inventory

COGS = 2×70 + 2×80 = 300. Ending = 2×80 + 1×100 = 260.

LIFO: newest costs → COGS; oldest costs → ending inventory. IFRS does not allow LIFO.

Same units: COGS = 1×100 + 3×80 = 340. Ending = 2×70 + 1×80 = 220. Those are the amounts in the comparison table.

NRV = sales price − costs to sell. Report min(cost, NRV), item by item.

Car: 16,000 − 500 = 15,500. Write-down = 18,000 − 15,500 = 2,500. Notebooks: 1,000 × (250 − 200) = 50,000.

Inventory turnover = Cost of goods sold ÷ Average inventory

Average inventory = (beginning + ending) ÷ 2

Days’ sales in inventory = 365 ÷ Inventory turnover

Costco: 101,065 / 8,682 = 11.64 times, and 365 / 11.64 = 31.4 days. Sears 3.82 and 95.6 days. Caterpillar 3.08 and 118.5 days.

Part 3 · Chapter 7 PPE and Intangible Assets

Capitalized cost includes invoice price, sales tax, legal fees, transportation, and installation. A cash discount reduces cost. Interest on the purchase is expensed.

Truck: (15,000 × 0.98) + 396 = 15,096.

Lump-sum cost assigned to an asset = (that asset’s fair value ÷ total fair value) × price paid

Land 1,000,000 / 4,000,000 = 25% × 3,600,000 = 900,000. Building = 2,700,000.

Depreciable cost = cost − residual value

Book value = cost − accumulated depreciation

Van: depreciable cost = 62,500 − 2,500 = 60,000.

Straight-line expense = (cost − residual) × 1 / useful life

60,000 × 1/3 = 20,000 each year. Mimi: (230,000 − 30,000) / 5 = 40,000.

Units-of-production rate = (cost − residual) ÷ estimated total units

Expense = rate × actual units this period

Van: 60,000 / 100,000 = 0.60 per mile. Year 1: 0.60 × 30,000 = 18,000. Mimi: 200,000 / 5,000 = 40 per unit × 1,200 = 48,000.

Double-declining expense = beginning book value × (2 / useful life)

Do not subtract residual inside this formula. In the last year, set book value equal to residual and back into the expense. Van year 1: 62,500 × 2/3 = 41,667. Kenneth Jay rate = 2 × 1/5 = 40%.

Goodwill = purchase price − fair value of identifiable net assets

Identifiable net assets = identifiable assets at fair value − liabilities at fair value

Valley Drug: 400,000 − (340,000 − 20,000) = 80,000. Not amortized.

Amortization of a definite-life intangible = cost ÷ useful life, straight-line, usually with no residual

Patent: use 8 years of useful life, not 13 years of remaining legal life. 200,000 / 8 = 25,000. License: 40,000 / 5 = 8,000. An indefinite life is not amortized.

Recoverable amount = higher of (fair value − disposal costs) and value in use

Impairment loss = carrying amount − recoverable amount, if carrying amount is higher

Fitness center: carrying amount 450,000. Recoverable = higher of 230,000 and 375,000 = 375,000. Loss = 75,000. New depreciation = 375,000 / 15 = 25,000.

Gain (loss) on disposal = cash received − book value − cash paid to remove the asset

Computer sold for 600 when book value is 3,000: loss 2,400. Sold for 600 when book value is 0: gain 600. Scrapped at book value 3,000 with 300 removal cost: loss 3,300.

Fixed asset turnover = Net sales ÷ Average net fixed assets

Named in the Chapter 7 learning outcomes (“interpret the fixed asset turnover ratio”). The lecture slides do not show this formula and do not compute an example. A higher ratio means more sales per unit of net PPE. Average net fixed assets = (beginning net PPE + ending net PPE) ÷ 2. This is the standard definition of the ratio the outcome names, not a worked slide.

Part 4 · Chapter 8 Liabilities

Bond issue prices and mortgage payments will be given. The journal entries, and the split of a given payment between interest and principal, are still examined.

Accounts payable turnover = Cost of goods sold ÷ Average accounts payable

Average days to pay = 365 ÷ Accounts payable turnover

Starbucks 2023: 7.64, and 365 / 7.64 = 47.77 days.

Interest = principal × annual rate × time

1 November note: 100,000 × 12% × 2/12 = 2,000 accrued at 31 December. The next four months are 4,000. Cash at maturity = 106,000.

Warranty expense at sale = sales × estimated warranty cost percent

Sun: 50,000 × 4% = 2,000. Later repairs debit the provision, not the expense.

Mortgage interest this month = unpaid principal × annual rate × 1/12

Principal this month = given payment − interest this month

The payment is given. McGiven’s payment is 733.76. First interest = 100,000 × 8% × 1/12 = 666.67. First principal = 733.76 − 666.67 = 67.09. Computing the payment itself is not on the final.

Cash interest for a bond period = face × coupon rate × (months in the period / 12)

$1,000 at 8%: annual 80, semiannual 40, quarterly 20. Amazon: 100,000 × 10% × 1/2 = 5,000. The 10-year, 6% bond of face 1,000, interest semiannual: 30 per period.

Not examined — present-value factors for the bond price

Issue price = PV of principal + PV of the interest annuity, both at the market rate per period

Par, 5%, n = 4: 100,000 × 0.82270 + 5,000 × 3.54595 = 100,000. Discount, 6%: 100,000 × 0.79209 + 5,000 × 3.46511 = 96,535. Premium, 4%: 100,000 × 0.85480 + 5,000 × 3.62990 = 103,630. You will not be asked to calculate these.

Market rate = coupon → par. Market rate above coupon → discount. Market rate below coupon → premium.

When market rates rise, bond prices fall.

Interest expense = beginning book value × market rate per period

Discount amortized = interest expense − cash interest. Book value rises.

Premium amortized = cash interest − interest expense. Book value falls.

Discount, first period: 96,535 × 6% = 5,792. Amortization 792. Book value 97,327.

Premium, first period: 103,630 × 4% = 4,145. Amortization 855. Book value 102,775.

Book value = face − unamortized discount = face + unamortized premium

At maturity, book value equals face in all three cases, and the principal entry is debit bonds payable, credit cash, for face.

Times interest earned = (Net income + Interest expense + Income tax expense) ÷ Interest expense

Amazon: 2021 = 22.09, 2022 = (1.51), 2023 = 12.80. A higher ratio is preferred. Parentheses mean a negative ratio.
